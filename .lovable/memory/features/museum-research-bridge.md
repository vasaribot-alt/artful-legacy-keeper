name: Museum Research Bridge
description: Core framing for museum outreach — today's artists are tomorrow's art history; GARF preserves documentation museums will need for future research
type: feature
---

When approaching museums and research centers (Met/Lauder, RKD, etc.), frame GARF as the bridge between what research centers study (the art of the past) and what GARF protects (the documentation of today's artists, who are tomorrow's art history).

Key points:
- Today's artists are tomorrow's history. GARF preserves the documentation and documents for today's artists and makes it available to museums for future research.
- GARF does NOT produce catalogue raisonnés. Living artists compile their own; committees or foundations handle deceased artists. GARF provides infrastructure and long-term hosting.
- Much documentation now exists only in digital form on subscription platforms that may not outlast the artist.
- GARF makes material available to museums and researchers with the artist's or estate's consent.
- The ask is cooperation and access, not data transfer or system replacement.

This framing turned the Lauder Center's modernism focus from a mismatch into a shared mission: the Center studies yesterday's art; GARF ensures today's documentation survives to become tomorrow's primary source.
